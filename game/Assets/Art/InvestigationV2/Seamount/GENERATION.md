The runtime sprite is produced deterministically from that render by
`docs/local-reference/seamount-render/make_sprite.py`, run under Blender's Python:

1. Crop the non-transparent bounds with 24px padding: `(144, 55, 1474, 1017)`.
2. Premultiply alpha, resample both axes with a Lanczos-3 kernel to `600×434`,
   then divide alpha back out. Premultiplying matters: resampling straight alpha
   pulls the transparent surround into the silhouette as a dark fringe.
3. Save as an 8-bit RGBA PNG at maximum compression.

## Why the lighting is turned down

The original runtime sprite (SHA-256
`c91c7bbe748270ca4bf6ce21f6a7cb9bd2c6b2b3d8c39ed84d17bb480332fe72`)
used the script's full light rig. Its opaque rock pixels averaged relative
luminance `0.09175`, or **4.27×** the UI water reference `#0A2B3F`
(`0.02151`). The linear-channel mean converted back to sRGB was `#305B6A`.

`SEAMOUNT_LIGHT=0.32` brings the current sprite to relative luminance `0.01663`,
or **0.77×** the same water reference. Its linear-channel mean converted back
to sRGB is `#11262D`, an **81.9%** reduction from the previous runtime sprite.
`SEAMOUNT_FOG=0.6` remains a shaping control for camera-depth attenuation on the
far flank; no isolated fog-only percentage is claimed without a matched render.

Measurements include pixels with alpha > 0.85. Each sRGB channel is converted
to linear light using the WCAG transfer function, per-pixel relative luminance
uses coefficients `0.2126 / 0.7152 / 0.0722`, and the reported luminance is the
arithmetic mean of those per-pixel values. The displayed mean RGB is the mean of
the three linear channels converted back to sRGB.

Only the hero angle is included in the Unity project. The eight-angle turntable
and heightmap remain local reference outputs until a separate angle-control or
3D-terrain feature is approved.
